CROSS-BORDER DOCUMENTS · APOSTILLE

Apostille, legalisation and sworn translation: which comes first?

Documents crossing borders may need an Apostille, legalisation, translation or a combination of these. The proper route depends on the issuing authority, destination country and requirements of the receiving institution.

An Apostille certifies the origin-related formalities of a public document, not the truth of its contents, and does not replace a translation where one is required.

Three distinct procedures

Apostille

Under the 1961 Hague Convention, certifies the signature, capacity of the signatory and/or seal or stamp on an eligible public document.

Consular legalisation

May apply where the Hague system or an applicable exemption is unavailable. The precise steps depend on the authorities and destination.

Sworn translation

An interpreter or translator swears to the fidelity of a translation under the relevant procedure; this does not authenticate the original document.

Five checks before arranging documents

  1. Identify the issuing country, authority and required form of the original or copy.
  2. Confirm destination country, recipient and intended legal or administrative use.
  3. Check whether the Hague Convention, EU arrangements or other exemptions apply.
  4. Ask whether a certified or sworn translation is required and in which language.
  5. Identify the competent authority and agree timing, handling and delivery.

Frequently asked questions

Which Italian authority issues an Apostille?

For judicial/notarial documents this generally involves the competent Procura della Repubblica; other relevant administrative public documents generally fall within the competent Prefettura. Individual requirements vary.

Does an Apostille remove the need to translate?

Not necessarily. An Apostille does not translate the content of the underlying document.

Can an Italian authority apostille a document issued in China?

Normally the designated authority in the country of origin issues the Apostille, following that jurisdiction’s rules.
