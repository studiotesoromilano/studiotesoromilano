What a sworn translation does—and does not—establish

An Italian sworn translation follows a formal declaration and sworn-record procedure. Its role must be distinguished from authentication of the underlying document or acceptance by a particular authority.

  • The translation: The translator swears to the fidelity of the translation under the relevant procedure. Receiving the oath does not mean the court has independently certified the translation’s content.
  • The original document: Swearing the translation does not verify the authenticity of the original driving licence or civil/commercial document. Apostille or other authentication may be separately required.
  • Its use: For eligible non-EU/EEA licence holders, Art. 135 permits an IDP or official Italian translation alongside a valid original licence, subject to residence and other conditions; this does not guarantee a rental vehicle or validity in other countries.

Studio TesoroMilano provides legal-language support and agreed sworn-translation procedures. The document type, intended use, receiving authority and delivery terms must be checked case by case.

Driving in Italy with a non-EU licence: what Article 135 requires

Under Article 135 of the Italian Highway Code, eligible holders of a valid non-EU/EEA driving licence must carry the original licence together with either a qualifying International Driving Permit (IDP) or an official translation into Italian. Studio TesoroMilano assists with complete Italian translations and the relevant sworn-translation procedure.

1. Valid original licence and one supporting document

The law uses “ovvero” to mean “or” in this context: a qualifying IDP OR an official Italian translation, together with the valid original licence.

2. The one-year condition concerns residenza

The relevant time limit concerns registered residence in Italy; it is not simply measured from a first holiday, the issue of a visa or possession of a residence permit.

3. Not every “international licence certificate” is an IDP

A commercial multilingual booklet or uncertified translation should not be assumed to satisfy the legal definition of an IDP.

4. A sworn translation is an established official route

The Italian transport ministry describes a complete Italian translation, including the option of an asseverated translation sworn before a court clerk, notary or justice of the peace.

5. Rental acceptance and driving legality differ

A rental desk applies contractual rules; traffic enforcement applies driving legislation. A translation does not replace the original licence or guarantee rental acceptance.

6. Non-compliance may lead to penalties

Article 135(8) provides for administrative penalties when the required IDP or official translation is missing. Applicable amounts should be checked against the legislation in force.

7. Legal terms require contextual translation

Terms such as “ovvero” and the Chinese licence notation “长期” require context-sensitive interpretation; mechanical word-for-word translation can be misleading.

8. Temporary driving is not licence conversion

Sworn translation, legalisation or Apostille does not itself convert a foreign driving licence into an Italian driving licence.

Before you travel: verify licence validity, the legal nature of your IDP or translation, your Italian residence status, and the rental company’s document requirements.
